## Video capture tools for developers

Video Capture SDK .NET is a C# library for recording video from USB webcams, IP cameras over RTSP and ONVIF, desktop screens, and Blackmagic Decklink capture cards on Windows, macOS and Linux. The cross-platform engine, VideoCaptureCoreX, is built on GStreamer and runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, Android and iOS. The classic VideoCaptureCore engine uses DirectShow and runs on Windows only.

Recordings go to MP4 with H.264 or HEVC video and AAC audio, to WebM with VP8, VP9 or AV1, or to MKV and AVI. Hardware encoding uses Intel Quick Sync, NVIDIA NVENC or AMD AMF when the GPU supports it.

For example, a WPF or MAUI app can preview a webcam, record it to MP4, and stream the same feed over RTMP or SRT in one session. The SDK handles device enumeration, format selection and audio source management, so application code stays focused on its own logic. Installation starts with the VisioForge.DotNet.VideoCapture NuGet package.

### VIDEO CAPTURE PIPELINE

During the preview or capture stages, video and audio streams can be processed with various effects. Afterward, these streams are split and directed to encoders and audio/video renderers for further processing.

### OUTPUT FORMATS

The SDK supports a wide range of popular video, audio, and image formats and codecs, such as MP4 (using H264/HEVC for video and AAC for audio), WebM (using VP8/VP9/AV1 for video and Vorbis or OPUS for audio), as well as WMV, MP3, JPEG, GIF, and PNG.

### VIDEO PROCESSING

You can add text, images, or GIF logos, adjust brightness, contrast, saturation, or hue, resize, crop, or rotate content, and apply chroma-keying or various video effects to enhance your content.

Video capture in C# takes about a dozen lines with Video Capture SDK .NET; recording a webcam to MP4 shows the whole path. Install the VisioForge.DotNet.VideoCapture NuGet package and call VisioForgeX.InitSDKAsync() once at startup. Create a VideoCaptureCoreX bound to a VideoView, take a camera from Video\_SourcesAsync() and assign it to Video\_Source as VideoCaptureDeviceSourceSettings; a fixed resolution and frame rate come from the camera's VideoFormats list.

Pass new MP4Output("output.mp4") to Outputs\_Add, assign a microphone from Audio\_SourcesAsync() to Audio\_Source via CreateSourceSettingsVC() with Audio\_Record set to true, and call StartAsync(); StopAsync() closes the file. On Windows the video is encoded to H.264 on the GPU encoder the SDK finds (NVIDIA NVENC, Intel Quick Sync or AMD AMF), or with OpenH264 when there is none, and the audio to AAC.

The preview keeps running in the same VideoView while the file is written, in WPF, WinForms, MAUI and Avalonia apps alike. The [save webcam video guide](/help/docs/dotnet/videocapture/guides/save-webcam-video/) adds WebM output, snapshots and the redistributable packages for each platform.

## Connect to an RTSP or ONVIF IP Camera in C#

Video Capture SDK .NET connects to an RTSP or ONVIF IP camera with one call: RTSPSourceSettings.CreateAsync(uri, login, password, audioEnabled) probes the stream, reads its format and returns a source for Video\_Source.

Protocol-level RTSP libraries such as RtspClientSharp and SharpRTSP hand over encoded frames, leaving decoding, display and file writing to your code; here one VideoCaptureCoreX instance previews the camera and, with Outputs\_Add(new MP4Output("camera.mp4")), records it as well. UseGPUDecoder moves decoding to the graphics card, and LowLatencyMode cuts the default 500 ms latency buffer to 150 ms for live monitoring.

FallbackSwitch shows a still image or text when the stream fails; it decodes the stream itself, so UseGPUDecoder does not apply alongside it. To find cameras without typing addresses, DeviceEnumerator.Shared.ONVIF\_ListSourcesAsync discovers ONVIF devices on the local network.

The [RTSP camera guide](/help/docs/dotnet/videocapture/video-sources/ip-cameras/rtsp/) covers low-latency and troubleshooting settings, and the [camera directory](/help/docs/dotnet/camera-brands/) lists RTSP URL formats for Hikvision, Dahua, Axis and more than 50 other brands.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I capture webcam video in C#?

Install the VisioForge.DotNet.VideoCapture NuGet package, call await VisioForgeX.InitSDKAsync() once at application startup, then create a VideoCaptureCoreX instance and assign a webcam source. Call StartAsync() to begin preview or capture. The 30-day trial needs no license certificate; register one when deploying a licensed app.

### Can I record the desktop screen to MP4?

Yes. Set the video source to Screen and configure the capture area (full screen or a specific region). The SDK supports hardware-accelerated H.264/H.265 encoding to MP4 with configurable frame rate, bitrate, and audio capture from system output or microphone.

### Does the SDK support IP camera capture via RTSP and ONVIF?

Yes. The SDK supports RTSP, ONVIF, HTTP MJPEG, and other IP camera protocols. For ONVIF cameras, you get PTZ control, device discovery, and configuration. You can preview live streams, record to file, or process frames in real-time.

### What UI frameworks and platforms are supported?

The SDK supports WinForms, WPF, MAUI, Uno Platform, and Avalonia on Windows. Cross-platform builds work on macOS, Linux, iOS, and Android. Both .NET Framework 4.6.1+ and .NET Core 3.1+ (.NET 5–11) are supported.

### Is hardware-accelerated encoding supported?

Yes. The SDK supports GPU-accelerated encoding via NVIDIA NVENC, Intel Quick Sync Video, and AMD AMF. This significantly reduces CPU usage during capture and enables real-time recording at high resolutions including 4K.

### Can I capture from GigE Vision and USB3 Vision cameras?

Yes. The SDK includes native support for GigE Vision and USB3 Vision industrial cameras via the GenICam standard. You can configure camera parameters, trigger modes, and capture high-resolution frames for machine vision and inspection applications.

### Can I capture a webcam or IP camera in Unity?

Yes. Video Capture SDK .NET ships a ready-to-import .unitypackage. The VideoCaptureCoreX engine captures local webcams and microphones and connects to IP / RTSP / ONVIF cameras, rendering the live preview into a Unity Texture2D. Local webcam capture targets Windows and macOS; RTSP / IP cameras work on Windows, Android, macOS, and iOS.

### Can I record video to MP4 from a Unity app?

Yes. Beyond live preview, VideoCaptureCoreX records the captured camera and microphone to MP4 with hardware-accelerated H.264 / HEVC encoding (NVENC and Quick Sync on Windows, MediaCodec on Android, VideoToolbox on macOS / iOS) — all from inside your Unity build.

### Can Video Capture SDK detect objects or recognize faces from a live webcam or IP camera?

Yes. Register YOLOObjectDetectorBlock, ObjectAnalyticsBlock, or FaceRecognitionBlock with VideoCaptureCoreX.Video\_Processing\_AddBlock before StartAsync. Detection and recognition run on-device via ONNX Runtime on every captured frame, with results delivered through the block's own event and optional overlays drawn into the preview and recording.

### Can I add live captions or subtitles while recording video?

Yes. Register a SpeechToTextBlock with VideoCaptureCoreX.Audio\_Processing\_AddBlock before StartAsync. It transcribes the microphone locally with Whisper and Silero VAD, and can render live subtitles or write SRT/VTT files alongside the recording.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I embed a video player in a WPF application?

Install the VisioForge.DotNet.MediaPlayer NuGet package, call await VisioForgeX.InitSDKAsync() once at application startup, then create a MediaPlayerCoreX instance with your VideoView. Open a file or URL with OpenAsync(), then call PlayAsync(). The SDK handles rendering, format detection, audio output, seeking, volume, and playback speed.

### Does the SDK support MAUI and cross-platform playback?

Yes. The Media Player SDK works with .NET MAUI for cross-platform video playback on Windows, macOS, iOS, and Android from a single codebase. It also supports Avalonia, Uno Platform, and WinForms for additional platform flexibility.

### What video and audio formats are supported?

The SDK plays 500+ formats including MP4, MKV, AVI, WebM, MOV, WMV, FLV, and more. Audio formats include MP3, AAC, FLAC, OGG, and WAV. The cross-platform MediaPlayerCoreX engine uses GStreamer as its media pipeline; on Windows the classic MediaPlayerCore engine uses DirectShow with optional FFMPEG filters or VLC — ensuring broad codec compatibility without requiring end-users to install additional codecs.

### Can I play RTSP and network streams?

Yes. The SDK supports RTSP, RTMP, HTTP/HTTPS, HLS, UDP, and SRT network streams. You can play live camera feeds, streaming servers, and online media sources with low-latency playback and configurable buffering.

### Is there hardware-accelerated video decoding?

Yes. The SDK leverages GPU-accelerated decoding via DXVA2, D3D11, VideoToolbox (macOS), and VAAPI (Linux). This enables smooth playback of high-resolution content including 4K and 8K video with minimal CPU usage.

### How do I extract frames from a video file in C#?

Use the SDK's frame grabber feature to capture individual frames during playback or at specific timestamps. You can save frames as bitmap images, process them in memory, or use them for thumbnail generation. The SDK supports both synchronous and asynchronous frame extraction.

### Can I play video in Unity?

Yes. Media Player SDK .NET ships a ready-to-import .unitypackage. The MediaPlayerCoreX engine plays local files, URLs, and network streams (HTTP, HLS, RTSP) directly into a Unity Texture2D, with seek, pause, resume, volume, and playback-rate control. A single cumulative package targets Windows, Android, macOS, and iOS.

### How is this different from Unity's built-in Video Player?

Unity's VideoPlayer is limited to a small set of containers and codecs and offers little control over network streaming. MediaPlayerCoreX adds broad format support (H.264, HEVC, AV1, VP9), hardware-accelerated decoding (MediaCodec on Android, VideoToolbox on iOS/macOS), RTSP and HLS streaming, frame-accurate seeking, and trick-play — the same engine you use in WPF, MAUI, and Avalonia.

### Does Unity playback use hardware decoding?

Yes. On mobile, playback uses the platform hardware codecs — MediaCodec on Android and VideoToolbox on iOS. Frames upload to the GPU texture through a zero-allocation, double-buffered path for smooth video without per-frame garbage.

### Can Media Player SDK run object or face detection on a video file during playback?

Yes. Register YOLOObjectDetectorBlock, ObjectAnalyticsBlock, or FaceRecognitionBlock with MediaPlayerCoreX.Video\_Processing\_AddBlock before OpenAsync/PlayAsync. Detection and recognition run on-device via ONNX Runtime on every played frame, with results delivered through the block's own event and optional overlays drawn into the rendered picture.

### Can Media Player SDK generate subtitles for a video that doesn't have captions?

Yes. Register a SpeechToTextBlock with MediaPlayerCoreX.Audio\_Processing\_AddBlock before OpenAsync/PlayAsync (with Audio\_Play set to true). It transcribes the file's audio track locally with Whisper and Silero VAD, and can render live captions or write SRT/VTT files.

## What is a video editing SDK?

A video editing SDK is a library that adds timeline editing to your own application. Your code places clips on a timeline, trims them, adds transitions, titles and effects, previews the result and renders a new file.

Video Edit SDK .NET does this in .NET applications: desktop apps built with WinForms, WPF, Avalonia or .NET MAUI, console and server tools that render in batches, Android and iOS apps through .NET MAUI, and Unity projects.

Unlike running FFmpeg from the command line, the timeline, the preview and the progress events stay inside your process. Unlike editor SDKs for the browser, it does not run in a web page; a web product calls it on the server instead.

### SDK STRUCTURE

Video Edit SDK .NET has two engines. VideoEditCoreX is the cross-platform engine, built on GStreamer Editing Services, and runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, Android and iOS. VideoEditCore is the classic Windows engine, built on DirectShow.

Both take video, audio and image files: AVI, MP4, MPEG-1/2, WMV, QuickTime MOV, VP8/VP9 and AV1 video, WAV, MP3, Vorbis, WMA, AAC, Speex and Opus audio, BMP, PNG, GIF, JPEG, TIFF and WebP images, and any other format for which a decoder is installed.

VideoEditCoreX also reads clips and images from a .NET Stream, and every clip can be trimmed by a start and stop time when it is added. VideoEditCoreX writes 20 output formats; VideoEditCore can also stream the result over the network or send it to a Blackmagic DeckLink card.

### VIDEO EDITING PIPELINE

Editing happens on a timeline. On VideoEditCoreX, each Input\_Add call places a clip on it: Input\_AddAudioVideoFile for a clip with sound, Input\_AddVideoFile or Input\_AddAudioFile for one track, and Input\_AddImageFile for a still with a set duration.

Without an insert time a clip goes after the last one; with one, it lands at that point. Input\_AddVideoLayer adds a layer for multi-layer composition, such as a watermark over the main clip.

When Start is called, the engine decodes each clip, applies transitions, effects and text overlays, encodes the result and writes it in the format set in Output\_Format. If Output\_Format is left empty, the same timeline plays in the video view as a preview, and Position\_Set seeks within it.

Progress and errors arrive through the OnProgress, OnError and OnStop events.

### OUTPUT FORMATS

VideoEditCoreX renders to 20 formats through the Output\_Format property: MP4, WebM, MKV, MOV, AVI, DV, FLV, MPEG-TS, MXF, WMV, Ogg Theora and animated GIF for video, and MP3, M4A, FLAC, Opus, Ogg Vorbis, Speex, WAV and WMA for audio only.

MP4 uses H.264 or HEVC video with AAC audio, encoded in software or on the GPU with NVIDIA NVENC, Intel Quick Sync or AMD AMF; on Android and Apple devices the H.264 encoder uses MediaCodec and VideoToolbox when the device has them.

VideoEditCore on Windows adds FFmpeg-based outputs such as MPEG-1/2/4 and FLV, and can encode through any third-party DirectShow filter installed on the system.

### VIDEO PROCESSING

The SDK facilitates the integration of SMPTE-compliant transitions between videos to create smooth, professional-looking transitions. It also enables adding text, images, or GIF logos to your content. Developers can adjust brightness, contrast, saturation, or hue to fine-tune the visual aspects. Furthermore, the SDK supports various operations such as resizing, cropping, and rotating, affording precise control over the video's appearance.

### AUDIO PROCESSING

Add an equalizer to your audio processing capabilities with the SDK. This allows you to fine-tune audio frequencies for optimal sound quality. You can also normalize audio levels, increase volume, or enhance bass frequencies to achieve the desired output (normalization and bass on VideoEditCore). The SDK also offers a wide range of other audio effects.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I cut and merge videos programmatically in C#?

Create a VideoEditCoreX instance, add source files to the timeline with start and end timestamps for trimming, arrange clips in the desired order, set the output format, and call Start(). The SDK handles frame-accurate cutting, re-encoding, and audio synchronization automatically.

### What output formats does the Video Edit SDK support?

The SDK supports MP4 (H.264/H.265), WebM (VP8/VP9), AVI, WMV, MKV, MPEG-1/2/4, and GIF output. Audio can be exported as MP3, AAC, OGG Vorbis, OPUS (VideoEditCoreX), FLAC, and WAV. You can also configure custom encoding parameters for bitrate, resolution, and frame rate.

### Can I add effects and transitions between video clips?

Yes. The SDK includes 50+ built-in video effects such as blur, sharpen, and color correction, plus chroma key and picture-in-picture on VideoEditCore. You can add smooth transitions between clips, overlay text and images with customizable positioning, and apply real-time audio effects.

### Can I render edited videos in batch?

Yes. Build each timeline in code (clips, trims, titles, transitions) and render the jobs one after another or in parallel, for example to produce templated videos from a list of inputs. Each job has its own output format, resolution, frame rate and encoding settings. For FFmpeg-based transcoding on Windows, see Video Edit SDK FFmpeg .NET.

### Is hardware-accelerated encoding available for video editing?

Yes. The SDK supports GPU-accelerated encoding via NVIDIA NVENC, Intel Quick Sync Video, and AMD AMF. Hardware encoding dramatically speeds up video export operations, especially for high-resolution content like 4K video.

### What platforms are supported for video editing?

The SDK supports WinForms, WPF, MAUI, Uno Platform, and Avalonia on Windows. Cross-platform editing works on macOS, Linux, iOS, and Android. Both .NET Framework 4.6.1+ and .NET Core 3.1+ (.NET 5–11) are supported.

### Can I edit or render video in Unity?

Yes. Video Edit SDK .NET ships a ready-to-import .unitypackage. The VideoEditCoreX engine builds a timeline of clips with effects and transitions, previews it live into a Unity Texture2D, and renders the result to MP4 — all from inside your Unity build, on Windows, Android, macOS, and iOS.

### Can I render a timeline to MP4 from a Unity app?

Yes. After composing the timeline, set an MP4 output and start the VideoEditCoreX engine to render the finished movie to disk with hardware-accelerated H.264 / HEVC encoding (NVENC and Quick Sync on Windows, MediaCodec on Android, VideoToolbox on macOS / iOS).

### Can I build an Android or iOS video editor app in C#?

Yes. Create a .NET MAUI app, add the VisioForge.DotNet.VideoEdit package and the platform redistributables, and use VideoEditCoreX as on the desktop: append clips with Input\_AddAudioVideoFile, preview them in VideoView and render with MP4Output. The SimpleEdit sample on GitHub joins clips from the gallery into one MP4 on Android and iOS.

### How long does it take to integrate a video editing SDK?

The engine side is small. Add the VisioForge.DotNet.VideoEdit NuGet package and the redistributable package for each target platform, then create VideoEditCoreX, add clips, set Output\_Format and call Start.

The SimpleEdit sample for .NET MAUI joins clips from the gallery into an MP4 in about 250 lines of C#, UI handlers included, and the Video Join Demo X samples do the same in WinForms and WPF.

The 30-day trial is fully functional, so the whole integration can be built and tested before you buy.

### How does the video editing pipeline work?

Clips are placed on a timeline with the Input\_Add methods. When Start is called, VideoEditCoreX, built on GStreamer Editing Services, decodes each clip, applies transitions, effects and overlays, encodes the result and writes it in the output format you set. With no output format, the same timeline plays as a preview in the video view.

### Can I use Video Edit SDK .NET in a web browser?

No. The SDK runs inside .NET applications on Windows, macOS, Linux, Android and iOS, not in a browser. A web product can still use it on the server, for example in a background service that renders an uploaded set of clips to MP4 and returns the file.

## Pipeline Examples

### SIMPLE PLAYER

C#

View Code

The simple player pipeline uses the UniversalSourceBlock to read and decode the source file, the VideoRendererBlock to display video, and the AudioRendererBlock to play audio.

```csharp
// Create MediaBlocks pipeline
_pipeline = new MediaBlocksPipeline();

// Add file source
var fileSourceSettings = await UniversalSourceSettings.CreateAsync("video.mp4");
var videoStreamAvailable = fileSourceSettings.GetInfo().VideoStreams.Count > 0;
var audioStreamAvailable = fileSourceSettings.GetInfo().AudioStreams.Count > 0;

var fileSource = new UniversalSourceBlock(fileSourceSettings);

// Add video renderer
if (videoStreamAvailable)
{
    var videoRenderer = new VideoRendererBlock(_pipeline, VideoView1);
    _pipeline.Connect(fileSource, videoRenderer);
}

// Add audio output
if (audioStreamAvailable)
{
    var audioOutputDevice = (await DeviceEnumerator.Shared.AudioOutputsAsync())[0];
    var audioOutput = new AudioRendererBlock(
        new AudioRendererSettings(audioOutputDevice));
    _pipeline.Connect(fileSource, audioOutput);
}

// Start playback
await _pipeline.StartAsync();
```

```csharp
var audioRenderer = new AudioRendererBlock(new AudioRendererSettings(audioRenderers[0]));

// Configure MP4 output
var output = new MP4OutputBlock(Path.Combine(
    Environment.GetFolderPath(Environment.SpecialFolder.MyVideos), "output.mp4"));

// Add video and audio tees
var videoTee = new TeeBlock(2, MediaBlockPadMediaType.Video);
var audioTee = new TeeBlock(2, MediaBlockPadMediaType.Audio);

// Connect everything
_pipeline.Connect(videoSource, videoTee);
_pipeline.Connect(audioSource, audioTee);

_pipeline.Connect(videoTee, videoRenderer);
_pipeline.Connect(audioTee, audioRenderer);

_pipeline.Connect(videoTee, output);
_pipeline.Connect(audioTee, output);

// Start MP4 recording
await _pipeline.StartAsync();
```

## Live Video Compositor

Build OBS-like live video production software with the Live Video Compositor — a real-time, multi-input, multi-output video composition engine built on top of Media Blocks SDK .NET.

Dynamically add and remove cameras, screen captures, media files, RTSP/IP camera feeds, and NDI sources as layered inputs, mix them with GPU-accelerated rendering, and simultaneously output to recording files and live streaming platforms.

Designed for multi-camera switching, picture-in-picture layouts, live overlays, and broadcast-quality audio mixing — all fully controllable from C# code at runtime.

The Live Video Compositor is a multi-input, multi-output composition engine built on the same pipeline model.

You create it with a canvas size and frame rate — LiveVideoCompositorSettings(1920, 1080, new VideoFrameRate(25)) — then add each source as an input with its own destination rectangle and Z-order: a camera filling the background, a screen capture as a picture-in-picture overlay, a media file, an RTSP camera or an NDI feed.

Inputs and outputs are added and removed with Input\_AddAsync and Output\_AddAsync while the composition is running, and each input runs in its own isolated pipeline, so one failing source does not stop the others.

Layers are mixed with GPU-accelerated OpenGL rendering with a CPU fallback, and the result can go to several destinations at once — an MP4 or WebM recording, a YouTube or Facebook live stream, a DeckLink card and an on-screen preview.

### Dynamic Inputs & Outputs

Add or remove video and audio sources and output destinations at any time — even while the compositor is running. Each input operates in its own isolated pipeline, so one failing source never disrupts the entire composition.

### GPU-Accelerated Mixing

Composite multiple video layers in real time using cross-platform GPU-accelerated rendering (OpenGL) or CPU fallback. Position each source with pixel-precise rectangles, control Z-order stacking, and choose from letterbox, stretch, or fill resize policies.

### Record + Stream + Preview

Output to multiple destinations simultaneously — record to MP4 or WebM, stream live to YouTube and Facebook, send video to Decklink hardware, and display an on-screen preview — all from a single compositor instance running in real time.

### Live Overlays & Effects

Apply text overlays, image watermarks, and post-composition video effects dynamically — no pipeline rebuild required. Real-time audio VU metering provides professional-grade level monitoring during live production.

### Live Video Compositor Setup in C#

C#

View Code

```csharp
// Create the Live Video Compositor (GPU-accelerated)
var settings = new LiveVideoCompositorSettings(1920, 1080, new VideoFrameRate(25));
var compositor = new LiveVideoCompositor(settings);

// Add the first camera as a full-screen background layer
var camera = (await DeviceEnumerator.Shared.VideoSourcesAsync())[0];
var cameraSettings = new VideoCaptureDeviceSourceSettings(camera); // picks an HD format when available
var cameraInfo = new VideoFrameInfoX(cameraSettings.Format.Width,
    cameraSettings.Format.Height, cameraSettings.Format.FrameRate);
var cameraInput = new LVCVideoInput(
    "Camera", compositor,
    new SystemVideoSourceBlock(cameraSettings), cameraInfo,
    new Rect(0, 0, 1920, 1080), autostart: true);
await compositor.Input_AddAsync(cameraInput);

// Start compositing — sources and outputs can be added/removed at any time
await compositor.StartAsync();

// Dynamically add screen capture (Windows) as picture-in-picture overlay
var screenSettings = new ScreenCaptureDX9SourceSettings
{
    Rectangle = new Rect(0, 0, 1920, 1080),
    FrameRate = new VideoFrameRate(25)
};
var screenInfo = new VideoFrameInfoX(1920, 1080, screenSettings.FrameRate);
var screenInput = new LVCVideoInput(
    "Screen", compositor,
    new ScreenSourceBlock(screenSettings), screenInfo,
    new Rect(1400, 50, 500, 300), autostart: true);
screenInput.ZOrder = 1;
await compositor.Input_AddAsync(screenInput);

// Dynamically add MP4 recording output while compositing is running
// (the camera is video-only, so the AAC track carries the compositor's audio mix)
var mp4Output = new LVCVideoAudioOutput(
    "Recording", compositor,
    new MP4OutputBlock(new MP4SinkSettings("recording.mp4"),
        new OpenH264EncoderSettings(), new AVENCAACEncoderSettings()),
    autostart: true);
await compositor.Output_AddAsync(mp4Output, start: true);

// Dynamically remove the screen overlay
await compositor.Input_RemoveAsync(screenInput.ID);

// Dynamically remove the recording output
await compositor.Output_RemoveAsync(mp4Output.ID);
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is Media Blocks SDK and how does it differ from other VisioForge SDKs?

Media Blocks SDK uses a modular pipeline architecture where you connect individual processing blocks (sources, encoders, effects, sinks) like building blocks. Unlike the Video Capture or Media Player SDKs that provide ready-made workflows, Media Blocks gives you full control over every stage of the media pipeline, making it ideal for custom or complex scenarios.

### How do I build a media pipeline in C#?

Create a MediaBlocksPipeline instance, add source blocks (file, camera, RTSP, screen capture), connect processing blocks (encoders, effects, overlays), and attach output blocks (file sink, renderer, network stream). Call StartAsync() to run. The SDK handles threading, synchronization, and hardware acceleration automatically.

### Is Media Blocks SDK .NET based on GStreamer?

Yes. Media Blocks SDK .NET is a commercial .NET SDK built on GStreamer. You work with typed blocks rather than the raw GStreamer API, and CustomMediaBlock adds any GStreamer element or bin description to a pipeline. The GStreamer libraries ship in the VisioForge.CrossPlatform.Core.\* NuGet packages on Windows, macOS, Android and iOS.

On Android the app also references a Java bindings project from the VisioForge samples repository. On Linux the SDK uses the distribution's GStreamer, version 1.22 or later (Ubuntu 24.04 ships 1.24), installed with the distribution's package manager, plus the VisioForge.CrossPlatform.Core.Linux.x64 or VisioForge.CrossPlatform.Core.Linux.arm64 package.

### Does Media Blocks SDK work on macOS, Linux, and mobile platforms?

Yes. Media Blocks SDK supports Windows, macOS 12+, Ubuntu 22.04+, Android 7.0+, and iOS 18+. It works with WinForms, WPF, MAUI, Uno Platform, and Avalonia. The same pipeline code runs across all platforms with platform-specific rendering and hardware acceleration.

### Can I stream video over RTMP, RTSP, or HLS?

Yes. The SDK includes dedicated streaming output blocks for RTMP (YouTube Live, Facebook Live), RTSP server, HLS, SRT, NDI, and Shoutcast. You can combine streaming with local recording and preview by using Tee blocks to split the pipeline into multiple outputs.

### Is GPU-accelerated encoding and decoding supported?

Yes. Media Blocks SDK supports hardware-accelerated encoding via NVIDIA NVENC, Intel Quick Sync, and AMD AMF, as well as hardware decoding. On mobile platforms, iOS and Android hardware codecs are used automatically. This enables real-time 4K processing with minimal CPU usage.

### Can I use industrial cameras (GigE Vision, USB3 Vision) and Blackmagic Decklink?

Yes. The SDK includes source blocks for GenICam-compatible industrial cameras (Basler, Allied Vision, FLIR/Teledyne) via GigE Vision and USB3 Vision. Blackmagic Decklink devices are supported with dedicated source and sink blocks for professional video capture and output.

### What is the Live Video Compositor and how does it differ from building pipelines manually?

The Live Video Compositor is a high-level real-time video composition engine built on top of Media Blocks SDK.

While you can assemble custom mixing pipelines manually with individual blocks, the compositor provides a complete architecture for OBS-like applications out of the box — including bridge-based input isolation, GPU-accelerated compositing, dynamic source and output management at runtime, Z-order layer control, live text and image overlays, post-composition effects, and audio VU metering.

It is ideal for building multi-camera switching systems, live streaming software, or broadcast production tools in C# and .NET.

### Can I use Media Blocks SDK in Unity?

Yes. Media Blocks SDK .NET ships a single, cross-platform .unitypackage for Unity 6 that targets Windows, Android, macOS, and iOS from one import. You get the complete SDK — capture, sources, decoders and encoders, effects, mixing, recording, and streaming — and render any pipeline into a Unity RawImage. Two sample scenes (a media-file player and a live RTSP / IP-camera viewer) are included as starting points. See the Unity documentation for setup and per-platform build guides.

### How is this different from Unity's built-in Video Player?

Unity's Video Player is fine for bundled clips, but it has no RTSP / IP-camera input, a limited codec set, and no capture or streaming. Media Blocks SDK adds live RTSP and IP-camera playback, a much wider range of formats and codecs (H.264, H.265, AV1, VP8/VP9, ProRes, MJPEG and more), device capture, recording, and outbound streaming — all through one consistent pipeline that behaves the same across Windows, Android, macOS, and iOS.

### Can I record or stream video from a Unity app?

Yes. From inside a Unity build you can record to MP4, MKV, or WebM and broadcast over RTMP, SRT, HLS, or a built-in RTSP server, using hardware-accelerated H.264 / H.265 encoders on mobile. Combine sources, overlays, and a video mixer to compose exactly what you record or stream — on desktop and mobile.

### Can I build AI video analytics pipelines in C#?

Yes. Media Blocks SDK .NET includes VisioForge.DotNet.Core.AI blocks for OCR, ANPR, object detection, object analytics, face recognition, background removal, and custom ONNX inference. You connect these blocks inside the same MediaBlocksPipeline as cameras, RTSP streams, renderers, encoders, file outputs, and streaming outputs.

### Does Whisper speech-to-text run offline?

Yes. The VisioForge.DotNet.Core.AI.Whisper package runs Whisper locally through the GGML/whisper.cpp backend and can use optional Silero VAD for speech segmentation. It can generate live subtitles, recognized speech events, and side-car SRT or VTT subtitle files without sending audio to a cloud service.

### Can I use custom ONNX models?

Yes. OnnxInferenceBlock lets you run a custom ONNX Runtime model over video frames and receive raw tensor outputs for your own decoder or post-processing code. For common detector families, YOLOObjectDetectorBlock already handles YOLOX, YOLOv8, and RT-DETR preprocessing, decoding, coordinate mapping, and overlays.

### Can I use these AI blocks with VideoCaptureCoreX or MediaPlayerCoreX instead of a manual pipeline?

Yes. Every AI block also implements the processing-block interfaces used by VideoCaptureCoreX and MediaPlayerCoreX, so you register the same block instances with Video\_Processing\_AddBlock or Audio\_Processing\_AddBlock before StartAsync (capture) or OpenAsync/PlayAsync (playback) — no manual MediaBlocksPipeline required.

```csharp
//   VisioForge.DotNet.VideoEdit
//   VisioForge.DotNet.Core.Redist.VideoEdit.x64
//   VisioForge.DotNet.Core.Redist.FFMPEG.x64   (FFmpeg.exe)

using System;
using VisioForge.Core.VideoEdit;
using VisioForge.Core.Types.VideoEdit;
using VisioForge.Core.Types.Output;
using VisioForge.Core.Types.FFMPEGEXE;

// .NET 6+ console app (top-level statements), no preview window
await using var core = new VideoEditCore();
core.ConsoleUsage = true;
core.Mode = VideoEditMode.Convert;

// Source file: its video and audio streams
var input = @"C:\Video\input.avi";
if (!await core.Input_AddVideoFileAsync(input))
{
    Console.WriteLine("Cannot open " + input);
    return 1;
}

// The second argument keeps the audio in sync with the video of the same file
if (!await core.Input_AddAudioFileAsync(input, input))
{
    Console.WriteLine("Could not add the audio track of " + input);
}

// MP4 with H.264 + AAC. The profile picks the first available GPU encoder
// (Intel Quick Sync, AMD AMF, NVIDIA NVENC), else Media Foundation H.264.
var ffmpeg = new FFMPEGEXEOutput(DefaultsProfile.MP4_H264_AAC, networkStreaming: false);

// Optional: choose the encoder yourself, e.g. HEVC on an NVIDIA GPU
// ffmpeg.Video = new HEVCNVENCSettings();

core.Output_Format = ffmpeg;
core.Output_Filename = @"C:\Video\output.mp4";

var errors = 0;
core.OnProgress += (s, e) => Console.WriteLine("Progress: " + e.Progress + "%");
core.OnError += (s, e) => { errors++; Console.WriteLine("Error: " + e.Message); };

// With ConsoleUsage = true, StartAsync returns when the conversion has finished
if (!await core.StartAsync() || errors > 0)
{
    Console.WriteLine("Failed");
    return 1;
}

Console.WriteLine("Done: " + core.Output_Filename);
return 0;
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is Video Edit SDK FFmpeg .NET?

Video Edit SDK FFmpeg .NET is an FFmpeg-powered video editing and converting SDK for .NET developers. It enables video timeline editing, format conversion, effects processing, and encoding in C# and VB.NET applications. Built on the Windows VideoEditCore engine that encodes via FFmpeg.exe (FFMPEGEXEOutput), it supports 1000+ media formats and runs on Windows.

### How do I use FFmpeg in C# without building command lines?

Create a VideoEditCore instance, add the source file with Input\_AddVideoFileAsync and Input\_AddAudioFileAsync, and set Output\_Format to an FFMPEGEXEOutput object. Its typed properties choose the container (OutputMuxer), the video encoder (for example H264NVENCSettings or HEVCQSVSettings) and the audio encoder, and the SDK builds the FFmpeg.exe arguments for you. Subscribe to OnProgress and OnError, then call StartAsync; in a console app (ConsoleUsage = true) it returns when the conversion has finished. Raw FFmpeg flags can still be added through Custom\_AdditionalVideoArgs and Custom\_AdditionalAudioArgs.

### What formats and codecs are supported?

The SDK supports virtually any format available in FFmpeg, including MP4, AVI, MKV, WebM, MPEG, MOV, and FLV containers. Video codecs include H.264, H.265/HEVC, VP8, VP9, AV1, ProRes, and MPEG-1/2/4. Audio codecs include AAC, MP3, OGG Vorbis, FLAC, Speex, Opus, WAV, and WMA. Image formats like PNG, JPEG, BMP, and animated GIF are also supported for overlays.

### Does it support cross-platform development?

No. This FFmpeg-engine SDK is built on the Windows VideoEditCore engine (a DirectShow pipeline that encodes via FFmpeg.exe) and runs on Windows only (10 and 11, Server 2016+). If you need macOS or Linux support, use the cross-platform Video Edit SDK .NET, which is powered by the VideoEditCoreX (GStreamer) engine.

### What .NET frameworks are supported?

The SDK supports .NET Framework 4.6.1 and later, .NET Core 3.1, and .NET 5 through .NET 11. It works with WinForms, WPF, console applications, web backends, and Windows services. The SDK is distributed via NuGet for easy integration into any .NET project.

### Does it support GPU-accelerated encoding?

Yes. The SDK leverages FFmpeg's hardware acceleration support for encoding with NVIDIA NVENC, Intel Quick Sync Video, and AMD VCE/AMF. GPU-accelerated encoding significantly reduces processing time for H.264 and H.265/HEVC output, especially for HD and 4K content.

### Can I apply filters and effects during editing?

Yes. The SDK provides extensive video effects including image and text overlays, brightness/contrast/saturation/hue adjustments, deinterlacing, denoising, chroma-key (green screen), rotation, resize, crop, pan/zoom, and motion detection. Audio effects include equalization, normalization, and volume boosting. FFmpeg's full filter graph capabilities are available for advanced processing.

### How does it differ from Video Edit SDK .NET?

Both products share the Windows VideoEditCore engine, a DirectShow pipeline with a timeline. Video Edit SDK FFmpeg .NET is the Windows-only edition that encodes through FFmpeg.exe (FFMPEGEXEOutput); choose it when the job is transcoding and format coverage on Windows: 1000+ formats, AV1, VP9. Video Edit SDK .NET adds native DirectShow and Media Foundation encoders and the cross-platform VideoEditCoreX (GStreamer) engine for Windows, macOS, Linux, Android and iOS; choose it for editing apps and for any cross-platform target.

## How a Virtual Camera Works

A virtual camera is a software device that other applications open exactly as they open a USB webcam. With Virtual Camera SDK, your application feeds that device in one of two ways: a DirectShow graph that ends in the Virtual Camera Sink filter, or .NET code that pushes frames through VirtualVideoWriter. Either way, the frames travel through shared memory to the Virtual Camera Source filter, which is registered on the system as a video capture device. When Zoom, OBS Studio or a browser opens that device, it receives your frames.

What that means in practice:

-   Anything your application produces can become the camera picture: a video file, a capture device or your own filter in a DirectShow graph, or BGRA, BGR24 and YUY2 frames that your .NET code renders.
-   The virtual microphone works the same way: an audio sink in your graph, or PCM samples pushed through VirtualAudioWriter, become an audio capture device for other applications.
-   The camera offers YUY2 at fixed sizes from 320×180 to 4096×2160, landscape and portrait, at 1–60 fps. The frames you send are scaled to the size the receiving application picks.
-   When nothing is streaming, the camera shows an image you choose, or starts an application you specify, instead of a black frame.
-   The camera is visible to applications that list DirectShow devices. The Windows Camera app, the camera list in Windows Settings and UWP apps use Media Foundation only and do not show it.

One installation provides one virtual camera and one virtual microphone. Several applications can open the camera at the same time and receive the same picture.

Which programming languages are supported?

Does it work with Windows 11?

Yes, on Windows 10 and Windows 11, in 32-bit and 64-bit applications. The camera is a DirectShow device: Zoom, OBS Studio and Chromium-based browsers see it, while the Windows Camera app, the Settings camera list and UWP apps, which use Media Foundation only, do not.

Can multiple applications use the virtual camera simultaneously?

Yes. Several applications can open the virtual camera at the same time, and each receives the same picture. One installation provides one virtual camera and one virtual microphone.

Can I play a video file as a webcam?

Yes. Build a DirectShow graph that opens the file with a source filter and ends in the Virtual Camera Sink; Zoom, OBS Studio or a browser then show the file as the camera picture, and the Virtual Audio Card sink can carry its sound to the virtual microphone. The SDK documentation has this file-to-camera example in C# and C++. A .NET application that decodes or renders frames itself can push them through VirtualVideoWriter instead.

What is the Windows virtual camera?

Windows 11 has its own API for virtual cameras, MFCreateVirtualCamera in Media Foundation. Virtual Camera SDK is a DirectShow virtual camera: it runs on Windows 10 and Windows 11, adds a virtual microphone, and is fed from a DirectShow graph or from .NET code, while the Camera app, the Settings camera list and UWP apps do not list it.

Is Virtual Camera SDK a virtual webcam app for my PC?

No. It is an SDK for developers: the camera shows whatever your own application sends to it. Does it work on Windows on ARM?

Yes. The filters are built for x86, x64 and ARM64, and the ARM64 camera and audio filters ship in the VisioForge.DotNet.Core.Redist.VirtualCamera.arm64 NuGet package. ## C# example: encrypt a video and play it back

In the .NET SDKs, encryption is a property of the MP4 output. This example renders an encrypted MP4 with Video Edit SDK .NET, which takes the same MP4Output as Video Capture SDK .NET, and plays it with Media Player SDK .NET using the same key.

### C#

C#

Collapse

Copy

```csharp
using VisioForge.Core.Types.MediaPlayer;
using VisioForge.Core.Types.Output;
using VisioForge.Core.Types.VideoEdit;

// Encrypt: VideoEditCore here; VideoCaptureCore takes the same MP4Output
var mp4Output = new MP4Output
{
    Encryption = true,
    Encryption_Format = EncryptionFormat.MP4_H264_SW_AAC,
    Encryption_Mode = EncryptionMode.V9_AES256,
    Encryption_KeyType = EncryptionKeyType.String,
    Encryption_Key = "lesson-key"
};

await videoEdit.Input_AddVideoFileAsync("lesson-01-source.mp4");
await videoEdit.Input_AddAudioFileAsync("lesson-01-source.mp4");

videoEdit.Mode = VideoEditMode.Convert;
videoEdit.Output_Filename = "lesson-01.mp4";
videoEdit.Output_Format = mp4Output;
await videoEdit.StartAsync();

// Play: MediaPlayerCore with the same key
mediaPlayer.Source_Mode = MediaPlayerSourceMode.Encrypted_File_DS;
mediaPlayer.Encryption_KeyType = EncryptionKeyType.String;
mediaPlayer.Encryption_Key = "lesson-key";
mediaPlayer.Playlist_Clear();
mediaPlayer.Playlist_Add("lesson-01.mp4");
await mediaPlayer.PlayAsync();
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the Video Encryption SDK?

Video Encryption SDK is a Windows SDK of DirectShow filters that encode video to MP4 with H.264 video and AAC audio and encrypt both streams with AES-256. The key can be a password string, a file or binary data. The muxer filter encrypts, the demuxer filter decrypts, and seeking works in an encrypted file as in any other MP4. Existing H.264/AAC files and streams can be re-muxed into an encrypted MP4 without re-encoding. Additionally, GPU encoders from the DirectShow Encoding Filters pack can speed up encoding. For example, an e-learning product can ship course videos that play only in an application holding the key. Samples cover C#, C++ and Delphi. Video Capture SDK .NET, Video Edit SDK .NET and Media Player SDK .NET are already integrated with the SDK and need a separate license for it.

### What encryption algorithms are supported?

The SDK supports AES-256 (256-bit Advanced Encryption Standard) as the primary encryption algorithm and AES-128 for broader compatibility. Encryption keys can be provided as text strings, file paths, or binary data. The SDK encrypts both video (H.264) and audio (AAC) streams independently within an MP4 container.

### Can I seek within encrypted video files?

Yes. The SDK's decryptor (demuxer) filter fully supports seeking, so users can navigate to any position in an encrypted video file just like a regular MP4 file. This enables standard media player controls including fast-forward, rewind, and jumping to specific timestamps.

### Which programming languages are supported?

The SDK provides APIs for C# (.NET), VB.NET, C++, and Delphi. For .NET developers, the VisioForge.DirectShowAPI NuGet package provides convenient interface wrappers. The SDK is also integrated into Video Capture SDK .NET, Video Edit SDK .NET, and Media Player SDK .NET (separate license required).

### Can I encrypt live streams or only files?

The SDK can encrypt both pre-recorded files and live streams. The muxer filter works within a DirectShow filter graph, so it can encrypt output from any source — including live camera feeds, screen captures, and network streams. You can also re-mux existing H.264/AAC content without re-encoding for faster processing.

### Is the encryption compatible with standard media players?

Encrypted files require the SDK's decryptor filter to play back. Standard media players cannot open encrypted files without the decryptor component installed. This is by design — it ensures only authorized applications with the correct decryption key can access the protected content, making it suitable as a foundation for DRM systems.

### What are the differences between the trial and commercial versions?

The trial version is fully functional for 30 days and may display a watermark or an unregistered notice. Paid licenses include royalty-free distribution rights and email support. Annual licenses include 12 months of updates; Lifetime·Team licenses include minor and major updates for the product lifetime.

### Are there export restrictions on AES-256 encryption?

AES-256 encryption may require export licensing or compliance with regulations in some countries. The SDK also supports AES-128 as an alternative for regions with stricter encryption export controls. Contact VisioForge if you need guidance on encryption regulations or require alternative encryption algorithms for your target markets.

### How do I encrypt a video file in C#?

In Video Capture SDK .NET or Video Edit SDK .NET, create an MP4Output with Encryption set to true, choose EncryptionMode.V9\_AES256 or V8\_AES128, pass the key in Encryption\_Key with its type in Encryption\_KeyType, and start the capture or the render: the file is written as an encrypted MP4. In a DirectShow graph of your own, the Encrypt Muxer filter does the same and takes the key through its IVFCryptoConfig interface. The C# example on this page shows both encryption and playback.

### How do I play or decrypt an encrypted video?

In Media Player SDK .NET, set Source\_Mode to MediaPlayerSourceMode.Encrypted\_File\_DS and set Encryption\_Key and Encryption\_KeyType to the key the file was encrypted with. In a DirectShow graph, the Decrypt Demuxer filter takes the key through IVFCryptoConfig and passes the H.264 and AAC streams on to the decoders. The key must be the same one used for encryption.

### Can I protect course videos for online classes?

Yes, when the lessons are watched in your own Windows application. Encrypt each lesson to MP4 with a key, distribute the files, and play them in an app built on Media Player SDK .NET or on the decryptor filter, which supplies the key. The SDK does not include a web player, a streaming server or key management: how keys reach the students' app is decided by your application.

### Can encrypted videos play in a browser or on a phone?

No. The files play only through the SDK's decryptor filter, a DirectShow filter that runs in Windows applications. Browsers, Android and iOS cannot open them.

## What is a video fingerprint?

A video fingerprint is a compact digital descriptor of a video's content: its colors, scene structure and the motion of objects. Video Fingerprinting SDK extracts one from a video file, a recorded TV broadcast or a live stream, and the result takes about 1 KB per second of video. Fingerprints can then be stored, copied and compared without the original files. A match survives changes in resolution, bitrate, encoding and file format, and a short fragment can be found inside a longer video. Indexing one second of video takes about 300 milliseconds, and comparing two 10-second fingerprints takes 1–2 milliseconds. For example, a TV clipping service can find every airing of a client's ad in a day of recorded broadcasts. The SDK is available for .NET on Windows, macOS and Linux and for C++ on Windows and Linux, and the .NET version includes a MongoDB helper for fingerprint storage.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What operating systems are supported?

• C++ SDK supports Windows and Linux, or any other POSIX-compatible operating system (available by request)

• .NET SDK supports Windows 10 and later, macOS 12 or later, and Linux

### What is the size of a video fingerprint file?

Each second of a video stream analyzed requires about 1kb for its video fingerprint. Duplicate detection generally only requires 3-5 seconds of each video to be indexed. Indexing of full videos is required in order to identify a video that has been included inside another.

### How much time is required for computations?

Indexing one second of video takes about 300 milliseconds – the actual processing of the file accounts for nearly half of that time (150 milliseconds), while video decompression accounts for the rest. Matching a pair of 10-second-long fingerprints takes 1-2 milliseconds. These times were established by testing with an Intel i3 processor and FFMPEG filters for video compression.

### Is video reading or decoding software included?

• C++: No. You will need some other library (FFmpeg, for example, demo available) to implement video reading in your application

• .NET: Yes. Decoding is built in, through GStreamer
